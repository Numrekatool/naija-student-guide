How to Identify a Genuine Scholarship Website in Nigeria

Finding a scholarship online can reduce the cost of education, but not every scholarship advert is genuine. Some websites and social-media accounts are created to collect application fees, steal personal information, or trick students into visiting harmful links.

Before you submit your details, upload documents, or send money, take time to verify the opportunity. This guide explains how Nigerian students can identify a genuine scholarship website and avoid common scholarship scams.

What is a scholarship scam?

A scholarship scam is a false offer designed to deceive students. The person behind it may pretend to represent a university, government agency, charity, company, or international organisation.

The scammer may promise a large award, free education, or a guaranteed place. After gaining your trust, they may request an application fee, processing fee, “clearance” payment, bank details, passwords, or copies of sensitive documents.

1. Find the original organisation behind the scholarship

Start by identifying who is offering the scholarship. A genuine opportunity should clearly state the organisation’s name, purpose, contact details, eligibility requirements, application dates, and official application process.

Do not rely only on a poster shared on WhatsApp, Facebook, Telegram, or a blog. Search for the organisation independently. Type its name into a search engine and look for its official website. Do not simply click the first link in a message.

If an announcement claims to come from the Federal Ministry of Education, visit education.gov.ng yourself and compare the announcement with the information on that official domain.

2. Check the website address carefully

Look at the website address before entering information. Be cautious when a message claims to represent a famous organisation but sends you to an unrelated domain.

Warning signWhy it matters
Misspelled organisation nameScammers often create look-alike websites.
Strange letters and numbersThe address may not belong to the claimed organisation.
Shortened link with no visible destinationYou cannot easily see where it leads.
Immediate payment or personal-data demandThe site may be designed for collection rather than application.

The padlock or https symbol does not prove that a website is genuine. Scam websites can also use secure connections. Verify the organisation, not just the padlock.

3. Never pay an upfront fee just to apply

Be extremely careful if a scholarship asks for an application fee, processing fee, redemption fee, clearance fee, verification charge, or payment to release the award.

The Federal Trade Commission advises students never to pay to apply for a scholarship and warns against offers that promise money in exchange for an upfront payment.

A message such as “Pay ₦5,000 today to secure your scholarship” is a major warning sign. So is a demand to send money to a personal bank account, mobile-money number, or individual wallet.

4. Be suspicious of guaranteed scholarships

Real scholarship programmes usually have eligibility rules, a closing date, a selection process, and a limited number of awards. Treat these statements as serious warning signs:

  • “Everyone who applies will receive money.”
  • “Your scholarship is guaranteed.”
  • “You have already won.”
  • “You were selected even though you never applied.”
  • “Pay now or your place will be given to someone else.”

5. Confirm the opportunity through a second source

Look for independent confirmation on the organisation’s official website, verified social-media accounts, university announcements, your school’s guidance office, or a recognised government portal.

Search the organisation’s name together with words such as “scam,” “complaint,” “fraud,” or “review.” A pattern of complaints, payment demands, fake testimonials, or unanswered questions should make you stop.

6. Protect your personal information

Never share your email password, banking PIN, one-time password, ATM card details, or social-media password. Do not upload sensitive documents until you have verified the organisation and confirmed why the information is required.

7. Take your time

Scammers create urgency because they do not want you to investigate. If you feel rushed, close the page and return later. Ask a teacher, parent, school administrator, or trusted adviser to review the offer.

A real opportunity can be checked. A suspicious opportunity tries to stop you from checking.

Quick verification checklist

  • Is the organisation clearly identified?
  • Did I find the opportunity on an official or independently verified website?
  • Does the website address match the organisation?
  • Is there a clear eligibility process and deadline?
  • Am I being asked for a password, PIN, one-time code, or unnecessary bank information?
  • Have I verified the offer through a second source?

What to do if you suspect a scam

Stop communicating with the website or person and do not make another payment. Save screenshots, web addresses, phone numbers, messages, receipts, and account details.

If you already sent money, contact your bank or payment provider immediately. Change any password that may have been exposed, especially if you reused it elsewhere. Report the incident to the appropriate Nigerian authorities and the platform where the scam appeared.

Final advice

Begin with organisations you can identify and verify: universities, government ministries, recognised foundations, established companies, and official education portals. Use social media to discover opportunities, but do not treat a social-media post as proof that an opportunity is genuine.

Never pay simply because someone promises a scholarship. Verify the organisation, inspect the website address, confirm the information through a second source, and protect your passwords and banking details.
